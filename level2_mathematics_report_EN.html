Level 2 Mathematics: the bridge from arithmetic to algebra

Spanish ASE Math · City Colleges of Chicago · NRS 2–3 · 96 hours · 16 weeks

Support document for the professional development session · Prof. José M. Fernández, ABE/ASE Math Master Teacher · Harry S. Truman College

Purpose of this report

This document accompanies the professional development presentation on Level 2 mathematics. It brings together, in a single place, the characteristics of the level, the topics that make it up, its continuity with Level 1, its role in preparing students for Level 3, and a detailed description of the integrative final project, a new form of assessment in which students apply what they have learned in real time. It is intended as a reference for faculty before, during, and after the session.

1What Level 2 is

Level 2 is the second stage of a single four-level sequence. It corresponds to NRS levels 2 and 3 and occupies the space between the concrete arithmetic students bring with them and the algebraic thinking that is formalized later. In other words: it is the level where mathematics stops being only “counting and calculating” and becomes “reasoning with structure.”

2The identity of Level 2: three defining ideas

1 · The sign appears

Students work for the first time with the full number line: negative integers, absolute value, opposites, and the four operations with signs. Debt, sub-zero temperature, and depth below sea level stop being metaphors and become numbers to operate with.

2 · The order of operations is completed

The order of operations extends to powers, perfect square roots, nested parentheses, and fractions. PEMDAS becomes the connecting thread that supports every later calculation in the level.

3 · Mathematics becomes financial

With proportions, percentages, and simple interest, the subject connects directly to adult life: receipts, discounts, taxes, loans, savings, and depreciation. For an adult Spanish-speaking learner, this is the level where mathematics “is good for something” right away.

3The eight topics of the level

Content is distributed across eight major topics of twelve hours each, following the progression of the Topical Outline in the 16-week syllabus and the Student Learning Outcomes. Topics 1–3 build the signed-number system and PEMDAS; topics 4–6 develop proportional reasoning, percentages, and financial mathematics; topics 7–8 close with geometry, statistics, and the final project.

4The connecting thread: from the sign to PEMDAS

The first three topics are not isolated blocks: they form a single construction line. The full PEMDAS of Topic 3 is possible only because Topics 1 and 2 prepared it step by step. From Topic 4 onward, the order of operations is applied tacitly in every calculation of the level.

This design avoids the classic error of “teaching PEMDAS once and considering it learned.” Here it is installed as a habit, not as a memorized rule.

5Continuity with Level 1

Level 2 builds on what students already master and extends it with new content. The design rule is clear: what was learned in Level 1 is treated as consolidated and revisited only as a foundation; the new effort concentrates on the sign, on structure, and on application.

Assumed mastered (Level 1)

  • Integers, place value, reading, and rounding.
  • The four operations with positive integers (standard algorithm).
  • Decimals: reading, writing, comparing, and operating.
  • Fractions, mixed and improper numbers (+, −, ×, ÷).
  • Basic order of operations (without powers or roots).
  • Perimeter and area of rectangles and triangles.
  • Measurement conversions; bar and line graphs.

Level 2 adds or extends

  • Negative integers: the four operations with signs.New
  • Complex fractions with negative coefficients.
  • Exponents and perfect square roots.New
  • Full PEMDAS with powers and roots.New
  • Ratios, direct and inverse proportions, percentages.
  • Simple interest and depreciation.New
  • Circle (circumference and area with π) and composite figures.
  • Descriptive statistics, circle graphs, and simple probability.

6The financial block: mathematics for adult life

Topic 6 —simple interest, depreciation, and conversions— is especially relevant for the adult Spanish-speaking learner, who arrives in the classroom with real experiences of loans, savings, receipts, and household budgeting. It is also new content relative to Level 1.

The sample lesson: simple interest and depreciation

The level’s financial lesson illustrates the program’s approach well. It starts from vocabulary (principal, rate, time), formalizes the essential idea that interest is always a percentage of the principal, and only then introduces the formula I = C · r · t. Linear depreciation is presented as “the other side of the coin”: the same structure with the opposite sign. Students build year-by-year growth and book-value tables before using the formula directly, and they always work from authentic contexts: the savings account, the bank loan, the car that loses value.

7The integrative final project: applying in real time

The level culminates, in lesson 8.3, with an integrative final project. It is not one more exam: it is an interactive worksheet in which students bring together tools from several topics and apply them to their own data. The project grade is combined with the summative quiz to form the level’s final grade, and the project replaces the lesson’s asynchronous assignment.

What students do

They choose a personally meaningful theme —“My monthly budget” or “A week of my time”— and work through five sections:

The five sections of the integrative final project and what students produce in each.
SectionWhat the student produces
1 · Personal dataAt least five categories with their absolute values (in dollars or hours).
2 · Central tendencyAt least two measures (mean, median, range, or weighted mean) with written work shown.
3 · Circle graphA sector graph labeled with name and percentage, with its central angles calculated.
4 · InterpretationAt least five complete sentences interpreting the graph and drawing a conclusion.
5 · Applied calculationA percentage (discount and tax) or a simple-interest calculation on their situation.

Why it is a new form of assessment

The worksheet calculates in real time as students type: when they enter their data in Section 1, the totals, percentages, and circle-graph angles update on their own, and an automatic check appears that students use to verify their own work. They see the effect of each data point immediately, but they must show their work in writing: the tool checks, it does not replace reasoning.

The result is an authentic close to the level that combines, in a single task, central tendency, graphical representation, written interpretation, and applied financial calculation. The worksheet saves work automatically in the browser and allows exporting to PDF. Submission accepts three formats —oral presentation, written forum, or a document in the assignment dropbox— and is assessed with a five-criterion rubric, which respects different modes of expression (the UDL principle of multiple means of action and expression).

8Preparing for Level 3

Each new Level 2 content area seeds a competency that Level 3 takes up and expands. The level does not end in itself: it prepares the ground for algebra and formal geometry.

9Pedagogical notes for the instructor

  • Context before symbol. Each topic opens with a real situation —thermometer, debt, receipt, budget— before formalizing. The symbol comes after the experience.
  • 50/50 distribution. Each lesson suggests 2 synchronous hours and 2 asynchronous: 48 and 48 in total.
  • TI-30XS calculator. Fully introduced for percentages, interest, and geometry with π. Always remember to clear the memory before calculating.
  • Alignment with Steck-Vaughn. Each lesson can be linked to the pages of the GED Mathematics workbook for physical-book reference.
  • UDL design. Authentic contexts, automatic checks, multiple submission formats, and accessible materials in Brightspace with Ally compliance.
